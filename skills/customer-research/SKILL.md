# Customer Research Skill

## Goal

Turn accessible evidence into a transparent summary of audience needs, objections, alternatives, and testable hypotheses—without pretending to have conducted interviews or surveys that did not happen.

## Workflow

1. **Define the question.** Identify the decision the research should inform, the audience/market, and what evidence would change the decision.
2. **Create a source plan.** Prefer primary sources: official product pages, public documentation, public reviews, public community discussions, published studies, and user-provided materials. Record URLs, dates when available, and access limitations.
3. **Collect evidence responsibly.** Use public, permitted pages. Respect platform rules, robots, rate limits, privacy, and access controls. Do not scrape private communities, personal data, or gated content.
4. **Keep quotes faithful.** Use short direct quotes only when actually visible in the source; preserve wording and link the source. Otherwise paraphrase and label it as a paraphrase. Never fabricate customer statements or imply a public post represents all customers.
5. **Code observations.** Group evidence into needs, triggers, desired outcomes, objections, alternatives, buying criteria, and vocabulary. Track source count and note when the sample is small or biased.
6. **Distinguish evidence from hypotheses.** For each theme, state supporting sources, counterevidence, confidence, and what remains unknown. Avoid false precision and invented market sizing.
7. **Translate into decisions.** Produce an evidence-backed audience profile, messaging implications, product questions, and a list of cheap tests that could validate uncertain hypotheses.
8. **QA.** Verify each cited source, remove duplicate evidence, check quote accuracy, and disclose coverage gaps.

## Output format

- Research question and scope
- Sources reviewed (URL, type, date if available)
- Key themes with evidence and confidence
- Audience needs, objections, and language
- Counterevidence and limitations
- Testable hypotheses and low-cost next steps
- Claims that must not yet be used in marketing

## Zero-cost and safety constraints

Use only public or user-authorized sources and existing free tools. No paid research APIs, subscriptions, fake interviews, fake survey responses, or fabricated quotations. Do not contact people or submit forms. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data; summarize patterns rather than profiling identifiable individuals.
